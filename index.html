Professor Harish-Chandra (1923 – 1983)

A Pillar of Modern Mathematics: Master of Representation Theory

Biography

A symbolic representation of mathematical structures

Early Life: Harish-Chandra was born on October 11, 1923, in Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh, India. He completed his higher education in physics from the University of Allahabad, where he also briefly worked under the legendary scientist Sir C. V. Raman.

Transition from Physics to Math: For his doctoral studies, he went to the University of Cambridge, where he earned his PhD under the guidance of the iconic physicist Paul Dirac. During his time at Cambridge, his interest shifted profoundly from theoretical physics to pure mathematics.

Magnificent Contributions: He spent the majority of his academic career at the **Institute for Advanced Study (IAS)** in Princeton, USA, where giants like Albert Einstein and Robert Oppenheimer were his contemporaries. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1973.

Key Research & Contributions

  • Representation Theory of Semi-simple Lie Groups: He constructed a foundational, revolutionary theory for the representations of Lie groups.
  • Harmonic Analysis: He successfully extended Fourier analysis to high-dimensional, complex algebraic and geometric structures.
  • Harish-Chandra's c-function: This crucial mathematical formulation plays a vital role in quantum mechanics and advanced algebra.
